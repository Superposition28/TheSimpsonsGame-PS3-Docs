Compiled Sound Bank Format (.sbk)

*.sbk is a compiled sound bank. Magic 6B 6E 62 73 = "knbs" → “sbnk” (sound bank) on a big-endian platform (PS3). At least some SBKs embed playable audio streams directly, alongside the bank metadata and playback configuration.

Internal Structure & Data

  • Header magic: 6B 6E 62 73 = k n b s → “sbnk” reversed by endianness (PS3 is big-endian). It’s a very common naming for “sound bank”.
  • Global tables → nodes: After the header you get a dense offset table (monotonically increasing pointers), then a series of variable-length node chunks starting ~0x01F0.
  • Audio-ish floats & defaults: Tons of BE floats: 41A00000 (20.0), 42C80000 (100.0), 3F800000 (1.0), 3E4CCCCD (~0.2), 3F333333 (~0.7)… exactly the kind of values you see in attenuation, pitch/volume, send levels, crossfades.
  • Per-node control fields: Repeating small enums/flags (… 03 00 04 01 … FF 00 …), plus IDs/hashes near each node. These align with event types/containers (one-shot, loop, random, sequence, switch), bus/category assignments, and ducking rules.

Verified Audio Payload

Running vgmstream-cli.exe -o test.wav .\bart_bc0_grp0_ss1_h0_ae0c63.sbk on the extracted bart_bc0_grp0_ss1_h0_ae0c63.sbk sample identifies its embedded stream data as follows:

  • Codec: Electronic Arts EA-XAS 4-bit ADPCM v1.
  • Layout: blocked Electronic Arts SNS; metadata is read from the Electronic Arts SBK header.
  • Bank contents: 289 selectable streams; the default stream index is 1.
  • Default stream: mono, 24,000 Hz, 18,947 samples (0.789 seconds), with a full-stream loop.
  • Playback duration reported by vgmstream: 277,894 samples (11.579 seconds) at 114 kbps.

This confirms that an SBK can be decoded directly as an EA SNS audio container. The exact meaning of the remaining tables, including whether they encode playback graphs, mix routing, or event metadata, remains unverified.

Auditory Survey

Most of the 157 WAV files produced by an SBK conversion were reviewed by listening. The reviewed material is predominantly sound effects rather than music or extended dialogue:

  • Character reactions: short screams, yells, and other vocal exclamations.
  • Impact effects: many very short breaking or collision sounds, with some substantially longer effects.
  • Action and object effects: sounds consistent with thrown objects, jumping, and other significant player or world interactions.

Working interpretation: this bank is likely used for reactive gameplay audio, particularly hits, impacts, and object interactions. Individual stream-to-event assignments remain unverified and should be confirmed by correlating stream IDs with in-game triggers or sound-event names.

Runtime Usage & Logic

The SBK appears to contain audio streams plus metadata that may define playback behavior:

  • Which event IDs exist for the hub (VO lines, UI beeps, ambience beds, props).
  • For each event/container: routing to mix buses, priority/virtualization, voice limits, randomization, looping, ducking, reverb sends, occlusion/obstruction response.
  • Curves for 3D rolloff, pitch/volume modulation, LFO/envelopes, crossfade/transition timings.

At runtime, when the game triggers an event:

  1. The engine selects an audio stream and any associated playback configuration from *.sbk.
  2. Any involvement of companion *.smb or *.exa files must be verified from additional samples.

Relationship to Other Formats

  • .sbk (this file): a bank that can embed EA SNS audio streams and metadata.
  • .smb and .exa: their relationship to SBK is not yet verified by this analysis.
